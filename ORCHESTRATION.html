When a transcript has one wrong word, should the fix retype the whole sentence, or just mark the word?

That question is the paper. Repair Without Rewrite compares two small AI models that correct transcripts. writes the whole corrected sentence. writes only a list of edits and lets a copier keep everything else exactly as it was. This page shows how far the paper has got, what runs next, and the ideas on the way.

In plain wordsA proofreader doesn't retype your essay to fix a typo. They mark the one word. The paper asks whether that habit is better for AI too: fewer new mistakes, and less work.

      Who does each job

      The frontier model is scarce, so it goes where a mistake would cost most, not to whatever feels most important. Three questions decide it.

      In plain wordsYou wouldn't ask a senior architect to lay bricks. You'd ask them to draw what nobody has drawn before, and to check the plans before the concrete is poured.

      Astra writes it

      There is no precedent in the repo to copy, and later work will build on the answer.

      Astra reviews it

      A precedent exists, but later work inherits the result, or it can't be undone. Codex writes; Astra reads the packet. Reviewing costs far less than writing, and that's how the quota stretches.

      Why the stops are worth it

      This project asks for patience: stop at the agreed lines, keep failures, don't let the implementer make scientific calls. The record shows what that has caught so far.

      In plain wordsEach of these was found before it could spoil a result. Every one would have looked like progress if nobody had checked.

      1. Practice mistakes that weren't word mistakes

        92.05% of the corruption table was punctuation, capitals and spacing. The single biggest entry, deleting a double quote, was 20.49% of all weight.

        docs/reports/EMPIRICAL_CORRUPTION_PROFILE_QUALIFICATION.md

        Caught before the training mix existed. Forty per cent of every trial run's practice would have been built from it.

      2. A reader recipe with gaps

        The approved shares (30/20/10/40) didn't say how examples are built, reused or counted. The implementer refused to fill those gaps itself.

        docs/reports/BC_10M_PROBE_ADMISSION_DECISION_V2.md

        Astra then wrote twelve sections of exact rules (A to L). Zero trial slots were spent finding out.

      3. Lower loss, broken output

        On the first tiny memorization test, B100 produced capped repetitions and C101 damaged an example that needed no change, even though the training loss fell. A longer, gentler rehearsal reached 4 of 4.

        docs/reports/BC_CORRECTNESS_REPORT.md

        This is why the plan judges models by complete outputs, never by loss alone.

      4. A speech recognizer typing nonsense

        Two of twelve clips came back as long strings of <unk>. Those two produced 3,090 of the 3,094 source errors. The cause was traced to a frame-bound defect and repaired.

        docs/reports/FOUNDATION_GO_NO_GO.md, question 16; docs/reports/PARAKEET_SOURCE_PATH_REPAIR.md

        Left alone, that would have made the "real" mistakes almost entirely garbage from two clips.

      5. A comparison model that only tidies up

        ByT5 produced valid output on 24 of 24 requests and made 0 useful corrections.

        docs/reports/BYT5_DEVELOPMENT_ADAPTATION.md

        A weak opponent would make either new model look good. It stays marked REPAIR_REQUIRED instead.

      6. Failures kept in plain sight

        Five scorer qualification attempts are kept on disk. A speech-synthesis path that failed its parity check stays in the record next to the path that worked.

        docs/reports/scorer_qualification_attempt01.txt to 05; docs/reports/ASR_TTS_NATIVE_PROBE.md

        A reviewer can see what was tried, not just what worked.

      7. A resume that didn't quite resume

        Stopping C101 mid-update and restarting it from disk failed to reproduce the uninterrupted run. The cause was the order in which its loss components were reduced; it was reproduced, repaired and re-qualified. Inconsistent checkpoint clocks are now rejected outright.

        Commits 04f3bec and 28298ad; experiments/manifests/lexical_reader_v2/
